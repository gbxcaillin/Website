# Marketing plan

A brand that no one meets doesn't grow. The marketing plan turns the positioning into a
demand engine: what we're trying to move, who we're moving, where we reach them, what we
say, what it costs, and how we know it worked. Build it top-down (objectives → strategy →
tactics), not bottom-up (a pile of tactics in search of a goal).

Fill `assets/marketing-plan-template.md`. Sections below, in order.

## 1. Objectives (SMART, tied to the business goal)
2–4 objectives, each Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Ladder each to
a business outcome. E.g. "Generate 120 qualified enquiries in H1" ties to "grow revenue
20%." Vanity metrics (followers, impressions) are not objectives; they're diagnostics.

## 2. Audience & funnel
- Restate the ICP / primary audience from positioning; add buying-committee roles for B2B.
- Map the funnel and the job at each stage. A useful default:
  - **Awareness** — they learn the brand/category exists (reach, education).
  - **Consideration** — they evaluate and trust you (proof, comparison, nurture).
  - **Conversion** — they buy/enquire (clear offer, low friction, strong CTA).
  - **Retention & advocacy** — they stay and refer (onboarding, value, referral).
- Identify the current bottleneck stage — spend where the funnel actually leaks, not where
  it's comfortable.

## 3. Channel plan
Don't list every channel — choose. For each candidate channel, judge: is the audience
there, does it fit the message and budget, and can the team sustain it? Better to own two
channels than dabble in eight.

For each chosen channel, specify: role in the funnel, content type, cadence, owner, and the
one metric that proves it's working. Common channels:
- **Website / SEO** — the hub; almost always foundational. Message clarity + conversion path.
- **Content / thought leadership** — articles, guides, talks, POV. Builds authority and SEO.
- **Email / CRM nurture** — the highest-ROI owned channel; sequence by funnel stage.
- **Organic social** — pick the platforms the audience actually uses (LinkedIn for B2B,
  etc.); consistency over presence-everywhere.
- **Paid media** — search (high intent), paid social (targeting/retargeting), display. Start
  small, measure CPA/ROAS, scale what works.
- **Referral / partnerships / introducers** — often the best B2B channel; formalise it.
- **PR / events / community** — credibility and relationships; slower, compounding.

State channels explicitly **out of scope** and why — a plan is choices, and saying no funds
the yeses.

## 4. Campaign calendar
A rolling calendar (quarter or 12-month) so activity compounds instead of firing at random.
- Anchor 2–4 **campaigns/themes** to the messaging pillars; each has a goal, offer, hero
  asset, and channel mix.
- Layer an **always-on** baseline (SEO content, nurture, social) beneath the campaigns.
- Note seasonality, launches, and events. Show it as a month-by-channel table.

## 5. Content engine
- **Pillars → formats → atomisation.** Each messaging pillar spawns a hero piece (guide,
  report, webinar) that's atomised into posts, emails, and snippets. Create once, publish
  many — this is how small teams sustain output.
- Cadence per format, and a simple production workflow (brief → draft → review → publish →
  repurpose). Note who owns each step.

## 6. Budget
- Allocate by objective and channel, not vibes. A common split is a majority to proven
  channels and a minority (10–20%) to test new ones.
- Separate working spend (media) from making spend (production/tools/people).
- If budget is tight, state the minimum viable plan and what you'd add with more — force the
  prioritisation.

## 7. Measurement & KPIs
- **North-star metric** the whole plan moves (e.g. qualified enquiries, MRR, pipeline).
- Per-channel KPIs and the leading indicators that predict them (so you can steer before
  results land). Distinguish leading (early) from lagging (outcome) metrics.
- Targets and cadence of review (weekly diagnostics, monthly against objectives, quarterly
  strategy). Name the tools (analytics, CRM, attribution) and any tracking to set up.
- Define what "not working" looks like and the pre-agreed response — kill, fix, or double
  down — so review meetings make decisions instead of admiring dashboards.

## 8. Team, tools & governance
- Who owns the plan; RACI for the main activities; in-house vs agency/freelancer split.
- Martech stack (CMS, CRM, email, analytics, scheduler, ad accounts) — what exists, what's
  needed.
- Brand governance: how new assets stay on-brand (guidelines, review step, templates).

## Compliance note
In regulated sectors, marketing claims carry legal weight. Keep offers and claims within
what the client can substantiate and is licensed to say; route anything near a regulated
line to compliance before it ships. Build the review step into the workflow, not as an
afterthought.

## Output
Produce the filled `assets/marketing-plan-template.md`, led by a one-page executive summary
(objectives, headline strategy, budget, expected outcome) an exec can approve on its own.
