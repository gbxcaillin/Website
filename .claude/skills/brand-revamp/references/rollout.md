# Rollout & launch

A revamp only counts once it's live, consistent, and measured. Rollout sequences the change
so nothing half-rebranded leaks out, the team can tell the new story, and you can prove the
revamp worked. Internal before external — a team that can't explain the new brand undermines
it on day one.

## 1. Sequence
1. **Finalise & govern.** Lock strategy, identity and guidelines. Freeze the source of
   truth so assets don't drift mid-rollout.
2. **Internal launch.** Bring the team along *before* the public sees it: the why, the new
   story, how to use the assets, the messaging house, the tone of voice. Give them the
   elevator pitches and the boilerplate. Their buy-in is the launch's foundation.
3. **Prepare assets.** Produce/update everything on the asset checklist against the new
   identity. Stage, don't publish.
4. **Soft launch.** Update owned, controllable channels first (website, email signatures,
   profiles). Sanity-check in the wild.
5. **Public launch.** Announce deliberately — a launch moment (post, email, PR, event) that
   tells the story, not a silent swap. Explain the change to existing customers so equity
   transfers instead of confusing them.
6. **Reinforce.** Roll the launch into the first campaign from the marketing plan so the new
   brand arrives with momentum, not a one-day spike.

## 2. Asset checklist (tailor to the client)
- **Digital:** website, favicon/app icons, social profiles + banners, email templates &
  signatures, newsletter, ad accounts/creative, landing pages, bio/link pages.
- **Sales & docs:** deck, proposal/quote templates, one-pagers, case studies, contracts
  letterhead, invoices.
- **Physical (if any):** signage, business cards, print collateral, packaging, uniforms,
  vehicle/branding.
- **Internal:** slide master, doc templates, email footer, intranet, Slack/Teams branding.
- **Legal/admin:** trademark filing (recommend professional counsel), domain(s), business
  name registrations, third-party listings and directories.

Assign each item an owner and a go-live date. Nothing "everyone owns."

## 3. Migration & continuity risks
- **Don't break what works.** Preserve SEO on a website change (redirects for changed URLs,
  keep metadata, avoid dropping ranking pages). Keep email deliverability (don't move domains
  carelessly — protect DNS/email records).
- **Redirect and reserve** old URLs, handles, and campaign links.
- **Grace period:** where a hard cutover is risky, run old and new briefly in parallel, but
  set an end date so the brand doesn't live in two worlds.
- **Communicate the change** to customers, partners and suppliers so invoices, logins and
  expectations still line up.

## 4. Measurement baseline
Capture the "before" so you can show the "after." Before launch, record baselines for the
north-star metric and key KPIs (traffic, enquiries, conversion, brand search volume,
sentiment). Set the review cadence (30/60/90-day checkpoints) and what success looks like at
each. Without a baseline, the revamp's impact is anecdote.

## 5. Launch communications
- **Internal announcement**: the story + the toolkit, delivered so the team feels ownership.
- **External announcement**: the narrative of the change (not "we have a new logo" but "here's
  what we now stand for"), across the launch channels.
- **FAQ**: for staff and customers — what changed, what didn't, what it means for them.

## Output

```
## Executive summary
## Launch sequence & timeline
## Asset checklist (owner + date)
## Migration & continuity risks (with mitigations)
## Measurement baseline & checkpoints
## Launch communications (internal, external, FAQ)
```
