# Brand audit

Purpose: an honest snapshot of where the brand stands before you change it, so the revamp
fixes real problems instead of chasing novelty. The output is a short written assessment
plus the 3–5 issues the revamp must solve. That issue list becomes the brief.

Audit across five lenses. For each, note what's working (equity to keep), what's broken,
and the evidence.

## 1. Perception — how the market sees them
- What do customers, prospects and lapsed customers actually say? (Reviews, sales calls,
  support tickets, testimonials, social mentions, NPS verbatims.)
- Is there a gap between how the brand sees itself and how the market describes it? Name it.
- What's the one-line reputation? Would the target audience even recognise the brand, and
  for what?

## 2. Verbal — what they say
- Is there a clear, differentiated value proposition, or category boilerplate anyone could
  claim? Test: could a competitor swap their name in and have it still read true? If yes,
  it's undifferentiated.
- Consistency of message across homepage, sales deck, social, email. Where does the story
  drift or contradict itself?
- Tone of voice: is there a recognisable voice, or does it read like anyone's?
- Claims audit (critical in regulated industries): list every claim of outcome, status,
  ranking, or capability. Flag any that overreach, can't be substantiated, or imply a
  licence/approval the client doesn't hold. These are risks, not wording nits.

## 3. Visual — how they look
- Logo: distinctive, legible at small sizes, works in one colour, dated or current?
- Colour: ownable and consistent, or default blue/grey? Accessible contrast?
- Typography: intentional and legible, or system defaults? Pairing coherent?
- Imagery: consistent style, real vs generic stock, on-strategy?
- Consistency across touchpoints (site, socials, docs, signage, email). Screenshot the
  worst offenders.

## 4. Experience — how it feels to interact
- Key journeys: find → evaluate → buy/enquire → onboard. Where's the friction?
- Website: clarity of message above the fold, mobile experience, speed, conversion path,
  broken deep links, contact/enquiry actually working.
- Off-site touchpoints: proposals, invoices, email signatures, physical spaces.

## 5. Market — the competitive and category context
- Map 4–8 competitors on two axes that matter to buyers (e.g. specialist↔generalist,
  premium↔value). Where is the brand now, and where is the open, ownable space?
- Category conventions: what does everyone in the space look/sound like? Sameness is
  opportunity — the space no one occupies is where differentiation lives.
- Trends and shifts (buyer behaviour, regulation, technology) that the brand must answer.

## Output format

```
# Brand audit — [Client]

## Executive summary
[3–5 lines: overall health, the core problem, the single biggest opportunity.]

## What's working (keep this equity)
- ...

## What's holding the brand back
1. [Issue] — evidence — impact
...

## Competitive map
[Two-axis map described in words + where the white space is.]

## The brief this creates
The revamp must: [3–5 must-fix outcomes, phrased as goals, not tactics].
```

Keep it candid. A flattering audit is useless; the client is paying for the truth.
