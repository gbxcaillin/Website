# Financial education: target audience and launch campaign

The financial education practice is a first-priority offer alongside
business performance and AI readiness. This document sets the audience, the
offer, the message and the first ninety days. It sits under the content
protocol (`docs/content-protocol.md`): everything here is education, not
financial product advice.

## 1. Who we are selling to, and why

Two audiences, both business buyers. Both keep the AFSL boundary easy to
hold, both use the leadership team's actual qualifications (two leaders with
financial planning and education degrees, one with brand and marketing), and
both are reachable through the network the firm already has.

### Primary: employers, as a workplace benefit

**The Financial Fluency Program.** Financial literacy workshops delivered to
staff as part of an employee wellbeing offer.

- Buyer: the People and Culture or HR lead, sometimes the owner or CFO.
- Organisation: 50 to 500 staff. Professional-services firms, healthcare
  and allied health groups, engineering and construction firms, education
  providers, local government and not-for-profits. Melbourne first, national
  by Microsoft Teams.
- Why they buy: financial stress is a recognised driver of absenteeism,
  presenteeism and turnover; wellbeing budgets exist and are under-used on
  money topics; super and pay questions land on payroll and HR who are not
  equipped or licensed to answer them; it is a visible, low-cost benefit
  that staff actually thank them for.
- Why it fits the licence position: the audience is employees, the content is
  factual literacy, no personal circumstances are collected, and the
  protocol's section 5 script handles "what should I do" questions.

### Secondary: advice and accounting practices, for their clients

**Client Education Series.** Independent educator delivers seminars or
webinars to a practice's clients and prospects; the practice hosts, invites
and, being licensed, does any advice afterwards.

- Buyer: practice principal or head of advice.
- Why they buy: client events are a proven retention and referral tool but
  advisers rarely have time to build and run them; an independent educator
  is more credible to a room than a pitch; it feeds their pipeline with
  warmer, better-informed conversations.
- Why it fits: GBX Professional Services educates, the licensee advises.
  Clean line, and the practice is already in the firm's network.

### Audiences considered and set aside for now

- Consumers direct (pre-retirees, first-home buyers): highest risk of
  drifting into advice, costly to acquire one by one, and paid seminars to
  the public need a strong brand first. Revisit after the employer channel
  produces case studies.
- Schools and universities: valuable, low willingness to pay, long
  procurement. Consider as pro-bono brand work later.
- Culturally and linguistically diverse communities: a genuine fit with the
  team's linguistics background and a real gap in the market; pursue through
  employer programs and community partners rather than as a paid channel.

## 2. The offer

### Program modules (all conceptual and factual, per the protocol)

1. **Money foundations.** Cash flow, budgeting that survives real life, the
   habits that matter more than the spreadsheet.
2. **Super, explained.** How it works, what the statement says, contribution
   types and caps in general terms, what to ask and where to look.
3. **Investing concepts.** Risk versus volatility, diversification, fees and
   compounding, the questions to take to a licensed adviser.
4. **Tax basics for employees.** How pay is taxed, what salary packaging is
   in principle, records and deductions in general terms.
5. **Protecting yourself.** Insurance concepts, scams and fraud, what to do
   when something goes wrong.
6. **Life moments.** The financial mechanics of buying a home, starting a
   family, career breaks, and later life, explained in principle.

### Formats

| Format | Length | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taster | 30 minutes, Teams | Free first touch, "Super in 30 minutes" |
| Lunch and learn | 45 minutes, on site or Teams | Pilot with one team |
| Workshop | 90 minutes | One module in depth, with Q&A |
| Program | 6 sessions over 6 to 12 weeks | The full Financial Fluency Program |
| Client seminar | 60 to 75 minutes | Advice and accounting practices |

Every session ends with the questions worth taking to a licensed adviser,
and the practice partner (where there is one) is introduced for that.

### Pricing structure (figures are yours to set)

- Taster: free.
- Single lunch and learn or workshop: fixed fee per session.
- Program: fixed fee for six sessions, with a per-head add-on above a
  headcount threshold.
- Client seminar: fixed fee per event, with an option for a quarterly series.
- Suggested anchor: price the program so that it costs the employer less per
  head than a single sick day, and say so in the sales conversation.

Record the decided figures in `docs/go-to-market.md` and set them in
`src/content.js` under `education.pricing` when you want them published.

## 3. Message

Program name: **The Financial Fluency Program**. Fluency, not literacy: it
says confidence and use, not remediation, and it nods to the team's language
background.

Positioning line for the employer audience:

> Financial education your people will actually thank you for. Plain-language
> workshops on money, super, investing concepts and life's big moments,
> delivered by educators, not product sellers.

For the practice audience:

> Client education that fills the room and warms the conversation. We teach,
> you advise.

Proof points to use everywhere: two leaders with financial planning
qualifications and education degrees; a written content protocol; no
products, no commissions, no licence to sell you anything; free tools on the
site that show the teaching style.

Words to avoid in all campaign material: advice, recommend, best, should,
returns, outperform, strategy (for individuals), any product or provider
name.

## 4. The funnel

1. **Free tool**: the Workplace Financial Wellbeing Check on the site. Eight
   one-tap questions about the organisation's current program, a tier and
   the gaps, email to receive results. Lead source for HR buyers.
2. **Free taster**: "Super in 30 minutes" on Teams, run monthly, open to any
   organisation. Attendees see the teaching quality; the HR contact sees
   staff engagement.
3. **Pilot**: one lunch and learn for one team at a fixed fee.
4. **Program**: the six-module Financial Fluency Program.
5. **Renewal**: annual refresh, new-starter sessions, a quarterly newsletter
   for participating employers.

For practices: free 30-minute conversation, then one seminar, then a series.

## 5. Channels

- **LinkedIn from the leaders' personal profiles.** Two posts a week for the
  first ninety days, alternating an education explainer (protocol-safe) and
  a behind-the-scenes note from a session. Target HR and People leaders and
  practice principals in the network with connection requests that reference
  the taster.
- **Direct outreach.** Twenty named target employers and ten practices. A
  short personal note offering the taster, not a pitch deck.
- **Partners.** Employee assistance program providers, payroll and HR
  platforms, industry associations for HR professionals, business chambers,
  and accounting and advice networks. Offer to run the taster for their
  members.
- **Site.** The Education page, the wellbeing check tool, education articles
  on the insights hub, and the newsletter.
- **Events.** Offer the taster as a free session at HR and wellbeing events
  and practice study groups.
- Paid: none for ninety days. Revisit once the taster fills without it.

## 6. Ninety-day calendar

| Weeks | Do |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 2 | Finalise module outlines and slides to the protocol. Set pricing. Build the target list of twenty employers and ten practices. Publish the Education page and the wellbeing check (this release). |

## 7. Assets to produce

- A one-page employer brochure (PDF) and a one-page practice brochure.
- The taster webinar deck and a registration page (use the contact form

- Post-session survey (five questions, no personal financial data).
- Email sequence for the wellbeing check: results, taster invitation,
  pilot offer.
- Ten LinkedIn posts drafted in advance.

## 9. Compliance guardrails for the campaign

- Every asset passes the protocol's four-question test and is logged in
  `docs/content-review-log.md` before it goes out.
- Sessions use the section 5 script for personal questions, and the
  facilitator says at the start that the session is education, not advice.
- No product or provider names in slides or handouts.
- Registration and survey forms collect name, email and organisation only.
- Practice seminars are introduced as education, with the licensed practice
  named as the party to speak to about personal advice.
